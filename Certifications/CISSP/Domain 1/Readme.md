This is one of the most important sections of CISSP Domain 1 because it teaches you **how management thinks about security**.

As someone working in Service Delivery and aiming for cybersecurity roles, this is a mindset shift from:

> "How do I fix this technical problem?"

to

> "How do I help the organization achieve its goals safely?"

That is the CISSP mindset.

* * *

# The Big Picture

## Restaurant Example

Imagine you own a restaurant chain.

Your goal is NOT:

-   Firewalls
-   Antivirus
-   Cameras
-   Passwords

Your goal is:

-   Make money
-   Keep customers happy
-   Grow the business
-   Protect the brand

Everything else supports those objectives.

Security exists to help the restaurant succeed.

Not to stop the restaurant from operating.

* * *

## Antivirus Company Example

Imagine you're the CEO of an antivirus company.

Your goals might be:

-   Gain 1 million new customers
-   Increase revenue by 20%
-   Protect customer data
-   Expand globally

As the security team, your job is NOT:

> "Block everything."

Your job is:

> "Help the company achieve those goals securely."

This single idea is what CISSP calls Security Governance.

* * *

# Corporate Governance

## What is it?

Corporate Governance is how a company is directed and controlled.

Think:

-   Goals
-   Direction
-   Oversight
-   Accountability

* * *

## Restaurant Example

The restaurant board decides:

-   Open 5 new stores
-   Increase profits
-   Improve customer service

Everyone in the company aligns to those goals.

The chefs cook.

The managers hire.

The finance team manages money.

Everyone moves in one direction.

That's Corporate Governance.

* * *

## CISSP Exam Thinking

When you see Governance:

Think:

> "How does management ensure the organization achieves its objectives?"

Not:

> "Which technology is best?"

Governance lives at the executive level.

* * *

# Security Governance

## What is it?

Security Governance ensures security supports business goals.

* * *

## Bad Security Team

CEO:

> "We want cloud services."

Security:

> "No."

CEO:

> "We want remote work."

Security:

> "No."

CEO:

> "We want AI."

Security:

> "No."

This is the "Department of No."

* * *

## Good Security Team

CEO:

> "We want cloud services."

Security:

> "Here are the risks and here's how we can reduce them."

CEO:

> "We want remote work."

Security:

> "We can deploy VPN, MFA and conditional access."

Security enables the business.

* * *

## Real ACT Government Example

Your business objective:

> Deliver services to ACT Government users.

Security objective:

-   MFA
-   Device compliance
-   Conditional Access
-   Endpoint security

These security controls support the business.

The security team is helping users work safely.

# Focus of Security

Most technical people get this wrong.

They think:

> Security exists to stop attacks.

Not exactly.

Security exists to:

> Help the organization achieve its objectives.

Stopping attacks is only one way of doing that.

# Enable Business

## Restaurant Example

Business goal:

> Online food ordering.

Bad security:

Good security:

Business succeeds safely.

# Accountability vs Responsibility

This is one of the most tested CISSP concepts.

* * *

## Restaurant Example

The restaurant owner owns the restaurant.

The kitchen manager runs the kitchen.

If the kitchen catches fire:

Who gets blamed ultimately?

The owner.

The owner remains accountable.

* * *

# Accountability

Think:

> Ownership

> Ultimate answerability

> The person whose name is on the line

Accountability CANNOT be delegated.

* * *

## Easy Memory Trick

### Accountability = Answerable

Both begin with A.

```
Accountability = Answerable
```

* * *

## Antivirus Company Example

CEO stores data in Azure.

Microsoft secures Azure infrastructure.

If customer data leaks:

Microsoft may have responsibility.

But the company remains accountable.

* * *

# Responsibility

Responsibility means doing the work.

Examples:

-   Administrators configure systems
-   SOC analysts monitor alerts
-   Microsoft manages Azure hardware

Responsibility CAN be delegated.

* * *

## Memory Trick

```
Accountability = Owns
Responsibility = Does
```

Who owns it?

Accountable.

Who does it?

Responsible.

# Due Care vs Due Diligence

Another CISSP favourite.

* * *

# Due Care

Due Care means:

> Doing the right thing.

* * *

## Restaurant Example

You install:

-   Fire alarms
-   Fire extinguishers
-   CCTV

You are protecting assets.

That's Due Care.

* * *

# Due Diligence

Due Diligence means:

> Proving you did the right thing.

* * *

## Restaurant Example

Inspector arrives.

You show:

-   Inspection reports
-   Maintenance records
-   Audit logs

That's Due Diligence.

* * *

## Easy Memory Trick

```
Due Care = Do

Due Diligence = Demonstrate
```

Or

```
Care = Action

Diligence = Evidence
```

Some countries may restrict:

-   Sale
-   Export
-   Distribution

of that technology.

# ITAR

Think:

> Defense

Missiles.

Weapons.

Defense systems.

Military technology.

* * *

## Memory Trick

```
ITAR = Arms
```

If it shoots, flies, explodes, or fights.

Think ITAR.

* * *

# EAR

Think:

> Dual Use

Used by civilians AND military.

Examples:

-   Encryption software
-   Advanced technology
-   Networking equipment

* * *

## Memory Trick

```
ITAR = Military

EAR = Everything Else Regulated
```

* * *

# Wassenaar Arrangement

Countries voluntarily share export-control information.

Think:

A club of countries saying:

> "Let's cooperate on controlling sensitive technologies."

* * *

# Memory Trick

```
ITAR = Law

EAR = Law

Wassenaar = Agreement
```

# Policies, Standards, Procedures, Baselines, Guidelines

This section causes confusion.

Let's use your workplace.

* * *

# Policy

Highest level.

Says WHAT must happen.

Example:

> All government laptops must have antivirus.

* * *

# Standard

Specifies WHICH solution.

Example:

> Microsoft Defender must be used.

* * *

# Procedure

Step-by-step instructions.

Example:

> Open Intune Deploy Defender Verify status

* * *

# Baseline

Minimum acceptable security.

Example:

Laptop must have:

-   Defender enabled
-   Firewall enabled
-   BitLocker enabled
-   Latest patches installed

* * *

# Guideline

Recommended.

Not mandatory.

Example:

> Users should reboot weekly.

Nice to have.

Not required.

* * *

# Easy Memory Trick

Imagine building a house.

```
Policy = We need a house.

Standard = Use brick.

Procedure = Steps to build.

Baseline = Minimum requirements.

Guideline = Recommended improvements.
```

# Procurement and SLA

## Restaurant Example

Restaurant wants a new food delivery service.

Before buying:

Security asks:

-   How is customer data stored?
-   Is data encrypted?
-   Where is data hosted?

That's procurement involvement.

* * *

# SLA

After choosing the provider:

Everything gets written into a contract.

Example:

Provider must:

-   Maintain 99.9% uptime
-   Encrypt data
-   Report breaches

That becomes the SLA.

* * *

## CISSP Exam Thinking

Remember:

```
Accountability stays with you.

Responsibility can move to vendor.
```

The SLA helps define responsibilities.

# Awareness vs Training vs Education

This appears regularly in CISSP questions.

* * *

# Awareness

Goal:

Make people aware.

Examples:

-   Posters
-   Emails
-   Phishing campaigns

* * *

## Restaurant Example

Poster says:

> Wash your hands.

Awareness.

* * *

# Training

Goal:

Learn specific skills.

* * *

Restaurant worker learns:

-   How to operate POS system
-   Food safety procedures

Training.

* * *

# Education

Goal:

Understand concepts.

* * *

Chef studies:

-   Nutrition
-   Culinary science
-   Hospitality management

Education.

* * *

## Memory Trick

```
Awareness = Know

Training = Do

Education = Understand
```

# Risk Management

The CEO has limited money.

Security has limited resources.

Risk Management answers:

> What should we protect first?

Not everything has equal importance.

* * *

## Restaurant Example

Would you protect:

-   Customer payment system
-   Decorative plant

The payment system.

Risk Management helps prioritize resources.

* * *

# The Most Important CISSP Lesson

When answering CISSP questions:

Do NOT think like:

-   Help Desk
-   SysAdmin
-   Network Engineer
-   Security Analyst

Think like:

-   Executive
-   Director
-   CISO
-   CEO

Ask yourself:

> Which answer best helps the organization achieve its business objective while managing risk?

Not:

> Which answer is the most technical?
